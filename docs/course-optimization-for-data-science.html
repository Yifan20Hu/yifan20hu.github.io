Optimization for Data Science.

16:960:690:01 / 16:954:694:02 · Rutgers University · Spring 2026

An in-depth graduate course on classical and modern optimization methods for statistics, machine learning, and data science.

Course Overview

The course begins with optimization models for learning from data and the theory of convex optimization. It then studies stochastic gradient methods, modern nonconvex optimization, and optimizers used in deep learning and reinforcement learning. Applications include statistical modeling, causal learning, robust optimization, and generative systems.

Emphasis is placed on modeling intuition, algorithm design, mathematical analysis, performance guarantees, and the limits of each method. Students learn to choose and assess optimization approaches for different applications.

Course Information

  • Cross-listed as 16:960:690:01 and 16:954:694:02.
  • Thursdays, 12:10–3:10 p.m., PH-111.
  • Prerequisites: solid analysis and linear algebra, familiarity with algorithms and computational complexity, and comfort with mathematical proofs.

Assessment and Project

One quiz contributes 20% of the grade and the course project contributes 80%. Five optional take-home questions can each add up to 5% bonus credit.

Students may work alone or in a group of two or three. Projects may develop an optimization algorithm, analyze one theoretically, or implement methods for an application. Students may select an instructor-provided topic or propose their own after discussion with the course team.
